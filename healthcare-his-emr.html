Hospital Information Systems & EMR

Integrated clinical data platforms using HL7 FHIR for interoperable healthcare delivery

Integrated Healthcare IT Systems for Clinical Excellence

Hospital Information Systems (HIS) serve as the digital backbone of modern healthcare facilities, orchestrating patient registration, appointment scheduling, billing, pharmacy management, laboratory workflows, radiology imaging, and clinical documentation. Electronic Medical Record (EMR) and Electronic Health Record (EHR) systems store comprehensive patient health histories, enabling clinicians to make informed decisions based on complete medical information rather than fragmented paper records.

Infrasys specializes in integrating disparate healthcare IT systems into cohesive digital ecosystems where patient data flows seamlessly across departments, facilities, and care settings. We implement Health Level Seven (HL7) standards and Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR) APIs that enable different EMR vendors, laboratory information systems, picture archiving and communication systems (PACS), and health information exchanges to communicate effectively.

Our HIS integration expertise spans multi-hospital networks, diagnostic chains, specialty clinics, and ambulatory care centers. We have integrated leading EMR platforms including Epic, Cerner, Allscripts, Meditech, athenahealth, and regional healthcare IT vendors with laboratory systems, radiology workflows, pharmacy dispensing systems, and insurance clearinghouses. Our solutions reduce duplicate data entry, minimize medical errors caused by incomplete information, and improve care coordination across the patient journey.

HL7 Messaging Standards & Integration Patterns

Health Level Seven (HL7) v2.x remains the most widely adopted healthcare integration standard globally, with billions of HL7 messages exchanged daily between hospital systems. Our HL7 integration expertise covers all major message types and trigger events:

  • ADT Messages (Admit, Discharge, Transfer): A01 (patient admit), A03 (patient discharge), A08 (update patient information), enabling real-time patient movement tracking across hospital departments
  • ORM Messages (Order Entry): Laboratory test orders, radiology imaging orders, medication orders, and procedure orders transmitted from EMR to departmental systems
  • ORU Messages (Observation Results): Laboratory results, radiology reports, vital signs, and diagnostic findings sent back to EMR for clinician review and documentation
  • DFT Messages (Detailed Financial Transaction): Billing and charge capture information for procedures, medications, and services rendered during patient encounters
  • SIU Messages (Schedule Information Unsolicited): Appointment scheduling, resource booking, and calendar synchronization across clinical departments
  • MDM Messages (Medical Document Management): Clinical document exchange including discharge summaries, consultation notes, and operative reports

We design HL7 integration architectures using enterprise integration engines like Mirth Connect, Rhapsody, Cloverleaf, and Ensemble that provide message routing, transformation, error handling, and monitoring capabilities. These integration platforms serve as the central nervous system for healthcare IT, orchestrating data exchange between dozens of disparate clinical and administrative systems.

FHIR: Modern Healthcare Interoperability

Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR, pronounced "fire") represents the next generation of healthcare data exchange standards, combining the best features of HL7 v2, HL7 v3, and CDA while leveraging modern web technologies like RESTful APIs, JSON, OAuth 2.0, and OpenID Connect. FHIR enables mobile health applications, patient portals, and health information exchanges to access clinical data with far less complexity than legacy HL7 integration.

FHIR Resource Implementation: We implement FHIR R4 resources covering the full spectrum of clinical and administrative data. Core resources include Patient (demographics and identifiers), Practitioner (clinician information), Encounter (hospital visits and consultations), Condition (diagnoses and problems), Observation (lab results, vital signs, clinical measurements), MedicationRequest (prescriptions), Procedure (surgical and diagnostic procedures), and AllergyIntolerance (medication and environmental allergies).

Our FHIR implementations follow US Core Data for Interoperability (USCDI) profiles mandated by ONC certification, ensuring compatibility with EHR systems certified under 21st Century Cures Act requirements. For international projects, we implement International Patient Summary (IPS) profiles enabling cross-border patient data exchange.

SMART on FHIR Applications: The Substitutable Medical Applications and Reusable Technologies (SMART) framework enables third-party applications to launch within EMR systems and securely access patient data using OAuth 2.0 authorization. We develop SMART on FHIR applications for specialized clinical workflows — decision support tools, patient engagement portals, population health analytics, and medical device integration — that work across multiple EMR vendors without custom integration for each.

EMR Vendor Integration Expertise

Healthcare organizations rarely use a single software vendor for all clinical systems. A typical hospital might use Epic for inpatient EMR, athenahealth for ambulatory clinics, a specialized oncology EMR for cancer treatment, Cerner for laboratory systems, and third-party platforms for radiology PACS, pharmacy dispensing, and revenue cycle management. Integrating these systems requires deep knowledge of vendor-specific APIs, data models, and implementation requirements:

Epic Interconnect Integration: Epic dominates the US hospital EMR market, serving over 250 million patients. We implement Epic Interconnect HL7 and FHIR interfaces, configure Chronicles database integration for real-time data access, and build MyChart patient portal extensions using Epic's App Orchard ecosystem. Our Epic-certified integration specialists handle complex scenarios like Haiku/Canto mobile clinician workflows and Care Everywhere health information exchange.

Cerner Millennium APIs: Cerner Millennium powers clinical workflows at thousands of hospitals worldwide. We leverage Cerner's FHIR APIs, CCL (Cerner Command Language) for custom reporting, and PowerChart physician documentation integration. Our solutions connect Cerner EMR with specialty systems for cardiology, radiology, and laboratory medicine while maintaining performance at high transaction volumes.

Open-Source EMR Platforms: For smaller hospitals, clinics in developing markets, and organizations seeking cost-effective alternatives to proprietary EMR vendors, we implement and customize open-source platforms like OpenMRS, OpenEMR, Bahmni, and GNU Health. These systems provide core EMR functionality with full source code access, enabling unlimited customization without vendor licensing fees.

Laboratory Information System (LIS) Integration

Clinical laboratories generate millions of diagnostic results daily — blood chemistry panels, microbiology cultures, pathology reports, molecular diagnostics — that must reach ordering clinicians quickly and accurately. Laboratory Information System (LIS) integration ensures that test results flow automatically from lab analyzers through the LIS into the EMR without manual transcription that introduces errors and delays:

We implement bidirectional HL7 ORU (results) and ORM (orders) interfaces connecting EMR systems with leading LIS platforms including Sunquest, Cerner Pathnet, Soft, Epic Beaker, and Meditech Laboratory. These interfaces handle order placement with correct billing codes, specimen tracking, preliminary and final results transmission, critical value alerting, and amended report distribution.

Modern laboratory integration extends beyond hospital-based labs to reference laboratories, molecular diagnostic companies, and point-of-care testing devices. We integrate national reference labs like Quest Diagnostics and LabCorp using their proprietary APIs, enabling community physicians to order specialized testing while keeping results in the patient's EMR longitudinal record.

Picture Archiving and Communication Systems (PACS)

Medical imaging represents one of the most data-intensive aspects of healthcare IT. A single CT scan can contain hundreds of megabytes of DICOM image files, and large radiology departments generate terabytes of imaging data annually. Picture Archiving and Communication Systems (PACS) store, retrieve, and distribute medical images to radiologists, referring physicians, and multidisciplinary care teams:

We design PACS integration architectures connecting imaging modalities (CT scanners, MRI machines, ultrasound, X-ray) with PACS storage, radiology information systems (RIS), and EMR viewing workstations. Our implementations use DICOM (Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine) standards for image transmission, HL7 for order and report messaging, and IHE (Integrating the Healthcare Enterprise) profiles like Scheduled Workflow, Patient Information Reconciliation, and Cross-Enterprise Document Sharing for Imaging.

Cloud-based PACS architectures enable multi-facility health systems to consolidate imaging storage, reduce hardware costs, and provide radiologists with remote reading capabilities. We implement vendor-neutral archives (VNA) that liberate imaging data from proprietary PACS systems, enabling long-term storage with migration flexibility as technology evolves.

Pharmacy Systems & E-Prescribing Integration

Medication safety depends on accurate communication between prescribers, pharmacists, and dispensing systems. E-prescribing integration connects EMR systems with pharmacy information systems, enabling clinicians to transmit prescriptions electronically, check for drug interactions, verify insurance formularies, and monitor controlled substance prescribing:

We integrate hospital pharmacy systems (Pyxis, Omnicell, BD HealthSight) with EMR platforms for automated medication administration record (MAR) workflows, IV room compounding, and bedside medication verification using barcode scanning. These closed-loop medication administration systems reduce wrong-drug and wrong-dose errors by ensuring that the medication scanned at the bedside matches the physician's order in the EMR.

For ambulatory e-prescribing, we implement NCPDP SCRIPT standard interfaces connecting EMR systems with Surescripts, the national e-prescription network serving 320,000+ pharmacies and 2.3 million prescribers across the United States. This enables real-time prescription benefit checks, medication history queries, and electronic prior authorization workflows that reduce administrative burden on clinical staff.

Health Information Exchange (HIE) Connectivity

Patients receive care from multiple providers across different health systems — primary care physicians, specialists, urgent care clinics, hospitals — each using different EMR systems. Health Information Exchanges (HIE) enable authorized clinicians to access patient records from external organizations, providing critical information during emergency care, specialty consultations, and care transitions:

We connect healthcare organizations to regional, state, and national HIE networks using Direct Secure Messaging (encrypted email for clinical documents), query-based document exchange (XDS.b profiles), and FHIR-based patient data access. Our implementations handle patient matching across different medical record numbers, consent management for data sharing, and audit logging of external record access.

The Trusted Exchange Framework and Common Agreement (TEFCA), established under 21st Century Cures Act, creates a nationwide interoperability infrastructure. We prepare healthcare organizations for TEFCA participation, implementing Qualified Health Information Network (QHIN) connectivity that enables seamless data exchange across previously siloed regional networks.

Clinical Decision Support Integration

Clinical decision support systems (CDSS) analyze patient data within EMR systems to provide real-time alerts, treatment recommendations, and evidence-based guidelines at the point of care. Effective CDSS integration can reduce medication errors, improve adherence to clinical protocols, and identify patients at risk for adverse events:

We implement CDS Hooks, a standard for integrating third-party clinical decision support into EMR workflows without custom interfaces for each vendor. CDS Hooks enable real-time alerts during order entry, suggesting alternative medications with lower cost or better efficacy, flagging potential drug interactions, and recommending evidence-based treatment protocols based on patient-specific factors.

Advanced clinical analytics leverage machine learning models trained on large EMR datasets to predict patient deterioration, identify sepsis risk, forecast hospital readmissions, and personalize treatment recommendations. We integrate these predictive models into clinical workflows using FHIR APIs and embed risk scores directly into physician dashboards and nursing workstations.
