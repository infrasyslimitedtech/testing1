Warehouse Management Integration

Smart WMS implementation and ERP integration for efficient distribution center operations

Enterprise Warehouse Management System Solutions

Modern warehouse operations demand precision, speed, and real-time visibility as order volumes grow and customer expectations for same-day or next-day delivery intensify. Warehouse Management Systems (WMS) have evolved from simple inventory tracking applications into comprehensive platforms orchestrating every aspect of distribution center operations — from receiving and putaway to picking, packing, shipping, and returns processing.

Infrasys specializes in implementing, integrating, and optimizing WMS platforms for e-commerce fulfillment centers, third-party logistics (3PL) providers, retail distribution networks, manufacturing warehouses, and cold storage facilities. Our implementations span leading WMS platforms including Manhattan Associates, SAP Extended Warehouse Management (EWM), Oracle WMS, Blue Yonder, Körber, and Infor WMS.

We understand that WMS selection and implementation is not purely a technology decision—it requires deep understanding of warehouse processes, labor management, automation integration, and seamless connectivity with upstream and downstream systems. Our approach combines business process optimization, change management, and technical integration to deliver measurable improvements in order accuracy, fulfillment speed, labor productivity, and inventory visibility.

Inventory Accuracy Through Automated Identification

Inventory accuracy is the foundation of effective warehouse management. Manual data entry and paper-based processes introduce errors that cascade through the supply chain—mis-picks, incorrect shipments, inventory write-offs, and dissatisfied customers. Our WMS implementations leverage automated identification technologies eliminating manual errors:

  • Barcode Systems: 1D and 2D barcode scanning for SKU identification, location verification, and shipment validation with real-time data capture at every transaction point
  • RFID Technology: Radio-frequency identification enabling hands-free scanning of multiple items simultaneously, ideal for high-velocity operations and serialized asset tracking
  • License Plate Numbers (LPN): Containerization strategies assigning unique identifiers to pallets, totes, and cartons for streamlined movement tracking throughout the warehouse
  • Vision Systems: Computer vision and AI-powered image recognition for automated dimensioning, damage detection, and quality control inspection
  • Voice Technology: Voice-directed picking and putaway enabling hands-free, eyes-free operations improving productivity and reducing training time for warehouse associates

These technologies integrate with WMS platforms providing real-time inventory visibility, automated cycle counting workflows, and exception-based management where supervisors focus attention on discrepancies rather than verifying expected results.

Order Fulfillment Optimization

The order fulfillment process—from order download to shipment dispatch—is the lifeblood of warehouse operations. Our WMS implementations optimize every step of this workflow:

Wave Planning: Intelligent order grouping based on shipping deadlines, carrier cutoff times, order characteristics, and warehouse zone capacity. Our wave planning algorithms balance workload across pick zones while maximizing carton consolidation opportunities reducing packaging materials and shipping costs.

Pick Path Optimization: Warehouse layout analysis and slotting strategies placing fast-moving SKUs in accessible locations. Pick path algorithms sequence pick tasks minimizing travel distance—serpentine routing for zone picking, batch picking for similar orders, cluster picking for multi-order carts.

Packing Optimization: Automated cartonization algorithms determining optimal box sizes based on item dimensions, fragility rules, and shipping carrier restrictions. Integration with dimensioning systems and shipping scales ensures accurate freight costing and compliance with carrier requirements.

Shipping Integration: Real-time connection with UPS, FedEx, DHL, and regional carriers for rate shopping, label printing, tracking number assignment, and automated shipment manifesting. EDI transactions (EDI 856 Advanced Ship Notice) notify customers and trading partners the moment shipments depart.

Labor Management & Productivity Analytics

Labor represents the largest operating expense in most warehouses. WMS-integrated labor management systems (LMS) provide visibility into workforce productivity, enabling data-driven decisions that improve efficiency while maintaining employee engagement:

Engineered labor standards define expected productivity rates for each warehouse task—receiving, putaway, picking, packing, replenishment—calibrated for warehouse layout, equipment, and item characteristics. Real-time performance tracking compares actual productivity against standards, highlighting both top performers and opportunities for coaching.

Incentive programs tied to productivity metrics and accuracy rates motivate warehouse associates while quality controls ensure speed doesn't compromise accuracy. Gamification elements—leaderboards, achievement badges, team competitions—create positive culture around continuous improvement.

Workforce analytics identify training needs, evaluate impact of process changes, and support capacity planning for peak periods. Predictive models forecast labor requirements based on order volume, enabling proactive scheduling decisions that avoid both understaffing and excess labor costs.

Warehouse Automation Integration

Modern warehouses increasingly incorporate automation technologies augmenting human labor with robotics, conveyors, and autonomous systems. Our WMS implementations integrate with diverse automation platforms:

  • Goods-to-Person Systems: Integration with automated storage and retrieval systems (AS/RS), vertical lift modules (VLM), and robotic shuttle systems bringing inventory to pick stations rather than workers traveling to locations
  • Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMR): WMS-directed robots transporting totes, pallets, and carts between zones eliminating unproductive travel time while human workers focus on value-adding picking and packing tasks
  • Sortation Systems: High-speed conveyor systems with barcode scanners and automated diverters routing packages to shipping lanes, reducing manual sorting labor and improving throughput
  • Automated Packing: Robotic carton erectors, void-fill dispensers, and tape applicators integrated with WMS packing workflows accelerating pack rates and standardizing packaging quality
  • Put Wall Systems: Batch picking with automated light-directed sorting into order containers, ideal for high-velocity multi-line order profiles common in e-commerce fulfillment

Automation integration requires tight synchronization between WMS and warehouse control systems (WCS) managing physical automation equipment. Our implementations establish robust interfaces ensuring order flow optimization across manual and automated zones.

Multi-Warehouse & 3PL Management

Companies with distributed warehouse networks face additional complexities—inventory allocation across locations, inter-facility transfers, order routing logic, and consolidated reporting. Our WMS implementations support multi-site operations:

Centralized order management with network-level inventory visibility enables intelligent order routing decisions—fulfilling from the warehouse closest to the customer, splitting orders across facilities when inventory constraints exist, or routing specialty items to facilities with appropriate handling capabilities (temperature control, hazmat certification).

For third-party logistics providers managing inventory for multiple clients, our WMS implementations provide client-specific configurations, segregated inventory pools, customized reporting, and billing integration supporting various pricing models (per-transaction, storage-based, value-added services).

Returns Management & Reverse Logistics

E-commerce growth has created a reverse logistics challenge—processing high volumes of customer returns efficiently while maximizing recovery value. Our WMS implementations streamline returns workflows:

Return authorization management captures customer-provided reason codes and photographs, generating receiving expectations pre-validated before items arrive. Upon receipt, quality inspection workflows determine disposition—return to stock, refurbish/repack, liquidate through secondary channels, or dispose/recycle.

Automated disposition rules based on product condition, return reason, and time since purchase accelerate processing while vendor chargeback workflows recoup costs for defective or damaged items through supplier agreements.
