HIPAA & GDPR Compliance

Secure, compliant health data platforms that protect patient privacy and meet regulatory requirements

Building Compliant Healthcare Data Infrastructure

Healthcare organizations operating in the United States must comply with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), while those serving European patients must adhere to the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). These regulatory frameworks impose strict requirements on how Protected Health Information (PHI) and personal health data are collected, stored, transmitted, and accessed.

Infrasys specializes in designing and implementing healthcare IT infrastructure that meets HIPAA Security Rule, Privacy Rule, and Breach Notification Rule requirements, as well as GDPR principles including data minimization, purpose limitation, and the right to erasure. Our compliance-first approach ensures that security controls are built into the architecture from day one, not retrofitted after regulatory audits identify gaps.

We work with hospitals, diagnostic centers, telemedicine platforms, health insurance companies, and healthcare SaaS providers to implement technical safeguards, administrative controls, and continuous monitoring systems that demonstrate compliance to regulators, auditors, and cyber insurance providers. Our solutions balance stringent security requirements with clinical workflow efficiency, ensuring that compliance controls enhance rather than impede patient care delivery.

HIPAA Technical Safeguards Implementation

The HIPAA Security Rule requires covered entities and business associates to implement technical safeguards that protect electronic Protected Health Information (ePHI) from unauthorized access, modification, or destruction. Our implementations address all required and addressable specifications:

  • Access Control (§164.312(a)(1)): Unique user identification, emergency access procedures, automatic logoff, and encryption/decryption mechanisms that restrict ePHI access to authorized personnel only
  • Audit Controls (§164.312(b)): Hardware, software, and procedural mechanisms that record and examine activity in information systems containing ePHI, including access logs, modification logs, and system activity monitoring
  • Integrity Controls (§164.312(c)(1)): Mechanisms to authenticate ePHI and detect unauthorized alterations, using cryptographic hash functions, digital signatures, and blockchain-based immutability where appropriate
  • Person or Entity Authentication (§164.312(d)): Multi-factor authentication systems combining passwords, biometrics, hardware tokens, or mobile authenticators to verify user identities before granting ePHI access
  • Transmission Security (§164.312(e)(1)): Technical safeguards to prevent unauthorized ePHI access during electronic transmission, including TLS encryption, VPN tunnels, and secure file transfer protocols

GDPR Compliance for Healthcare Organizations

The European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) classifies health data as "special category personal data" under Article 9, requiring additional protections beyond those for ordinary personal information. Healthcare organizations processing EU resident health data must demonstrate compliance with six lawful bases for processing and seven key principles:

Data Subject Rights Implementation: We design systems that enable healthcare organizations to respond to GDPR data subject access requests (DSAR) within the mandated 30-day timeframe. This includes the right to access (Article 15), right to rectification (Article 16), right to erasure/"right to be forgotten" (Article 17), right to restriction of processing (Article 18), right to data portability (Article 20), and right to object (Article 21).

Our DSAR automation platforms integrate with EMR systems, hospital databases, billing systems, and appointment scheduling platforms to locate all instances of a patient's personal data, generate comprehensive reports, enable selective data deletion while maintaining medical record retention requirements, and produce machine-readable data exports in structured formats like JSON or XML.

Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIA): For high-risk processing activities such as large-scale systematic monitoring of patient populations, use of new technologies like AI-powered diagnostic systems, or processing of genetic/biometric data, GDPR Article 35 mandates conducting Data Protection Impact Assessments before implementation. We facilitate DPIA workshops that identify privacy risks, evaluate necessity and proportionality of data processing, and design mitigation measures to reduce identified risks.

Encryption Architecture for PHI Protection

Encryption serves as the primary technical safeguard protecting PHI confidentiality. Our encryption architectures implement defense-in-depth strategies that secure data throughout its lifecycle:

Data at Rest Encryption: All databases storing PHI utilize transparent data encryption (TDE) at the storage layer, with additional application-level encryption for highly sensitive fields such as Social Security Numbers, credit card information, and genetic data. We implement column-level encryption using AES-256 in Galois/Counter Mode (GCM), with encryption keys managed through Hardware Security Modules (HSM) or cloud-based key management services like AWS KMS, Azure Key Vault, or Google Cloud KMS.

Data in Transit Encryption: All PHI transmission occurs over encrypted channels using TLS 1.3 with perfect forward secrecy (PFS) cipher suites. This applies to web applications (HTTPS), API communications, database connections, email containing PHI (S/MIME or PGP), and file transfers (SFTP or FTPS). We disable weak protocols like SSL 3.0, TLS 1.0, and TLS 1.1 that have known vulnerabilities.

Encryption Key Management: Cryptographic key lifecycle management follows NIST SP 800-57 guidelines for key generation, distribution, storage, rotation, and destruction. Master encryption keys are stored in FIPS 140-2 Level 3 certified HSMs, with automated key rotation every 90 days. Access to encryption keys requires multi-person control with cryptographic separation of duties.

Access Control & Identity Management

Healthcare environments require granular access controls that balance security with clinical urgency. A physician needs immediate access to patient records during emergency situations, while administrative staff should only view appointment and billing information. Our identity and access management (IAM) solutions implement least privilege principles with break-glass emergency access procedures:

  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Predefined roles (Physician, Nurse, Pharmacist, Billing Staff, Lab Technician) with specific permission sets, mapped to job functions and clinical workflows
  • Attribute-Based Access Control (ABAC): Dynamic access decisions based on user attributes (department, location, shift time), resource attributes (patient ward, data sensitivity), and environmental attributes (time of day, network location)
  • Context-Aware Access: Adaptive authentication that requires additional verification steps when accessing PHI from unusual locations, unfamiliar devices, or outside normal working hours
  • Privileged Access Management (PAM): Session recording, just-in-time access provisioning, and approval workflows for administrators who manage healthcare systems and databases
  • Break-Glass Access: Emergency access mechanisms allowing clinicians to override access restrictions during life-threatening situations, with all break-glass events logged, alerted, and subject to retrospective review

Audit Logging & Security Monitoring

HIPAA requires audit logs capturing all access to ePHI, but effective security monitoring goes beyond compliance checkboxes. Our audit logging architectures provide forensic investigation capabilities, insider threat detection, and real-time security alerting:

We implement centralized log aggregation using Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) platforms that collect logs from EMR systems, databases, web servers, firewalls, intrusion detection systems, and endpoint devices. All logs are timestamped using Network Time Protocol (NTP) synchronized to authoritative time sources, ensuring accurate event sequencing during incident investigations.

Our SIEM correlation rules detect suspicious patterns indicative of insider threats or external attacks: excessive PHI record access by a single user, access to patient records outside assigned departments, bulk PHI downloads, login attempts from geographically impossible locations, and repeated authentication failures followed by successful login (credential stuffing attacks).

Audit logs are stored in write-once, read-many (WORM) storage systems that prevent tampering or deletion. Log retention follows HIPAA's six-year minimum requirement, with options for extended retention to support legal discovery processes. All audit logs are encrypted and access-controlled, with administrative access logged to a separate tamper-proof audit trail.

Business Associate Agreement (BAA) Compliance

Cloud service providers, IT vendors, and SaaS platforms that process PHI on behalf of covered entities must sign Business Associate Agreements (BAA) accepting HIPAA obligations. We help healthcare organizations evaluate vendor BAA terms, conduct third-party security assessments, and implement technical controls that limit vendor access to the minimum necessary PHI:

When migrating healthcare workloads to AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud, we architect environments that maintain HIPAA compliance using cloud-native security services. This includes enabling AWS HIPAA-eligible services, configuring Azure Policy for HIPAA compliance, implementing Google Cloud Security Command Center, and maintaining detailed documentation demonstrating due diligence in vendor selection and oversight.

Breach Response & Notification Procedures

Despite preventive safeguards, breaches can occur through cyberattacks, insider threats, or accidental disclosures. HIPAA Breach Notification Rule requires notification to affected individuals within 60 days if a breach affects 500 or more people, and the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) must be notified. GDPR requires notification to supervisory authorities within 72 hours of becoming aware of a data breach.

We implement breach detection systems using anomaly detection algorithms, user behavior analytics (UBA), and data loss prevention (DLP) tools that identify potential breaches in real-time. Our incident response playbooks guide healthcare organizations through breach assessment (determining if the incident qualifies as a breach under HIPAA/GDPR definitions), containment procedures, forensic investigation, regulatory notification, and remediation activities.
