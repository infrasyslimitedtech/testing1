Edge Data Center Strategy

Distributed computing infrastructure for ultra-low latency applications and 5G services

Edge Computing Infrastructure for Telecom Operators

Edge data centers represent a fundamental shift in how computing resources are deployed and consumed. Rather than centralizing all compute and storage in hyperscale cloud regions hundreds of miles from end users, edge infrastructure positions compute capacity at network aggregation points — within tens of milliseconds of mobile subscribers, IoT devices, and enterprise applications.

For telecom operators, edge data centers unlock new revenue opportunities in 5G services, augmented reality, autonomous vehicles, industrial IoT, and real-time video analytics. These applications cannot tolerate the 50-100ms round-trip latency of centralized cloud — they require single-digit millisecond response times that only edge computing can deliver.

Infrasys specializes in designing and deploying edge data center infrastructure for telecom operators transitioning from connectivity-only business models to edge computing platform providers. Our solutions span site selection, facility design, power and cooling systems, network connectivity, edge orchestration platforms, and operational integration with existing central office and NOC environments.

Site Selection & Planning

Geographic analysis, latency modeling, real estate assessment, and capacity planning to identify optimal edge locations aligned with 5G coverage zones.

Facility Design & Build-Out

Micro-datacenter architecture, modular infrastructure deployment, prefabricated solutions, and retrofitting of existing central office space for edge compute.

Edge Computing Architecture for 5G Networks

The deployment of 5G Standalone networks creates a unique opportunity and requirement for edge computing. Unlike 4G LTE, where the packet gateway (PGW) was centralized in a few regional locations, 5G architecture distributes the User Plane Function (UPF) to the edge, enabling ultra-low latency and localized data processing.

Our edge data center designs support distributed UPF deployment, positioning compute resources within 10-20 milliseconds of radio access network (RAN) sites. This enables applications like cloud gaming, remote surgery, industrial automation, and autonomous vehicles that require guaranteed low latency and high reliability.

  • Multi-Access Edge Computing (MEC): ETSI MEC-compliant platforms that expose radio network information, real-time network analytics, and location services to third-party applications running at the edge
  • 5G UPF Co-Location: Hosting User Plane Functions within edge data centers to minimize latency between RAN and packet core, enabling network slicing and dedicated enterprise 5G services
  • Distributed CDN Nodes: Content delivery network caching infrastructure positioned at edge sites to reduce video streaming latency and backhaul bandwidth consumption
  • IoT Edge Gateways: Protocol translation, data aggregation, and local processing for massive IoT deployments in smart cities, manufacturing, and agriculture applications
  • AI Inference at the Edge: GPU-accelerated servers for real-time video analytics, facial recognition, predictive maintenance, and autonomous systems requiring local decision-making

Edge Facility Design & Infrastructure

Edge data centers differ fundamentally from traditional hyperscale facilities. Space is constrained, skilled technical staff may not be on-site 24x7, and environmental conditions (temperature, humidity, air quality) may be less controlled than purpose-built datacenters. Our designs account for these operational realities while maintaining the reliability that telecom services demand.

We design edge facilities across three deployment models, each optimized for different coverage areas, capacity requirements, and operational constraints:

Regional Edge Data Centers (100-500kW): Purpose-built facilities or build-outs within existing carrier hotels serving multiple cities or a large metropolitan area. These sites provide 500-2000 servers of capacity with full redundancy (N+1 power, dual-path cooling) and 24x7 on-site technical staff. Regional edge sites aggregate traffic from multiple far-edge locations and provide higher compute density for applications like video rendering, gaming servers, and enterprise virtualization.

Metro Edge Sites (20-100kW): Compact data centers within central office buildings, cellular switching centers, or colocation facilities serving a single city or dense suburban area. These sites typically house 100-500 servers in 5-20 racks with N+1 redundancy and remote management capabilities. Metro edge sites host 5G UPFs, MEC platforms, and localized caching for densely populated urban zones.

Far Edge Micro-DCs (2-20kW): Prefabricated, ruggedized enclosures deployed at cell tower aggregation sites, small cell hubs, or enterprise premises. These micro-datacenters contain 1-5 racks of compute and storage with integrated cooling and backup power in a weatherproof outdoor-rated enclosure. Far edge sites enable ultra-low latency for specific applications (factory automation, stadium experiences, campus networks) but with limited compute capacity.

Power Infrastructure & Energy Efficiency

Edge data centers must deliver datacenter-class power reliability within space and budget constraints that would be considered impossible in traditional facilities. We design power systems that balance reliability, efficiency, and capital costs while preparing for future density increases as workloads migrate to the edge.

  • High-Density Power Distribution: 400V DC or 415V 3-phase AC power distribution systems supporting 15-30kW per rack, enabling GPU servers and high-density compute without requiring excessive floor space
  • Modular UPS Systems: Right-sized uninterruptible power supplies with lithium-ion battery backup providing 10-15 minutes of runtime to bridge utility interruptions and enable graceful shutdowns
  • Backup Generators: On-site diesel or natural gas generators for extended outages, with automatic transfer switches and weekly exercise schedules to ensure operational readiness
  • Renewable Energy Integration: Solar arrays and battery energy storage systems (BESS) to reduce operating costs, meet corporate sustainability goals, and provide resilience during grid outages
  • Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE): Targeting PUE values of 1.3-1.5 through efficient cooling designs, free cooling when climate permits, and elimination of unnecessary power conversion stages

Cooling System Design for Space-Constrained Environments

Traditional raised-floor hot aisle/cold aisle cooling strategies often cannot be implemented in edge facilities due to ceiling height limitations, building structural constraints, and the need to retrofit existing telecom central offices. We design cooling solutions tailored to each site's unique constraints:

In-row cooling units positioned between server racks deliver chilled air directly into cold aisles, maximizing cooling efficiency in high-density deployments. Rear-door heat exchangers mount to the back of server racks, capturing hot exhaust air before it mixes with room air, enabling cooling within standard office environments without raised floors or overhead ducting.

For outdoor micro-datacenter deployments, we specify integrated cooling systems rated for extreme ambient temperatures (-40°C to +55°C) with air filtration to handle dusty or polluted environments near roadways and industrial sites. Liquid cooling solutions using direct-to-chip cold plates or immersion cooling enable densities exceeding 50kW per rack for AI/ML workloads in the smallest footprint possible.

Edge Orchestration & Management Platforms

Managing hundreds or thousands of geographically distributed edge sites requires automation and orchestration platforms that centralize lifecycle management, workload placement, and operational monitoring. We implement edge management solutions that integrate with existing telecom OSS/BSS systems:

Kubernetes Edge Distribution OpenStack Edge Computing ETSI MEC Framework Akraino Edge Stack VMware Telco Cloud Red Hat Edge Solutions

These platforms provide centralized orchestration, automated workload deployment, and remote lifecycle management across distributed edge infrastructure. Container-based deployments enable application portability and efficient resource utilization, while edge-native Kubernetes distributions handle the unique challenges of intermittent connectivity, limited bandwidth to central management systems, and autonomous operation during network partitions.

Site Selection & Geographic Coverage Planning

The value of edge computing depends entirely on positioning compute resources close to end users and devices. We use geospatial analysis and latency modeling to identify optimal edge site locations that balance coverage, capacity, and cost:

  • Latency Heatmaps: Modeling network propagation delay from candidate edge sites to population centers, enterprise districts, and industrial zones to ensure target latency SLAs are achievable
  • Coverage vs. Density Analysis: Balancing the number of edge sites (more sites = lower latency but higher capex) against compute density per site to minimize total cost of ownership
  • Traffic Demand Forecasting: Analyzing mobile network data, video streaming patterns, and enterprise application usage to predict edge compute demand by geography
  • Real Estate Evaluation: Assessing existing central office locations, colocation facilities, and greenfield sites for power availability, fiber connectivity, zoning compliance, and lease costs
  • Redundancy & Resilience: Designing edge site pairs with overlapping coverage areas to enable failover when maintenance or outages occur at individual locations

Edge-to-Cloud Connectivity Architecture

Edge data centers do not operate in isolation — they are part of a distributed computing continuum from IoT devices at the network edge to hyperscale public clouds hundreds of miles away. We design network architectures that optimize data flow across this continuum based on latency requirements, bandwidth costs, and regulatory constraints.

Workloads requiring sub-10ms latency (augmented reality, industrial control systems, autonomous vehicles) execute entirely at the edge. Applications needing moderate latency (video streaming, gaming, mobile app backends) process at regional edge sites with capacity to handle larger workload scale. Batch processing, data warehousing, and machine learning training occur in centralized cloud regions where economies of scale drive down compute costs.

We implement SD-WAN overlays and application-aware routing that automatically directs traffic to the appropriate tier of infrastructure based on latency, bandwidth, and cost policies. Direct cloud on-ramps at edge sites enable low-latency connectivity to AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud without traversing the public internet, reducing security risks and ensuring predictable performance.

Operational Integration & Service Assurance

Edge data centers must integrate with telecom operators' existing operational systems for provisioning, monitoring, fault management, and billing. We implement integrations that minimize operational friction and enable rapid service delivery:

  • OSS/BSS Integration: Service catalog integration for customer self-service ordering, automated provisioning workflows, and usage-based billing for edge compute services
  • Network Monitoring: SNMP, NetFlow, and IPFIX telemetry collection from edge infrastructure, integrated into existing NOC monitoring platforms for unified fault and performance management
  • Remote Hands Services: Contracts with national technical service providers for remote hands support at unstaffed edge locations, reducing the need for dedicated on-site staff
  • Automated Remediation: Self-healing capabilities that automatically restart failed services, rebalance workloads, and alert NOC staff only when human intervention is required
  • Capacity Planning: Proactive monitoring of compute, storage, and network utilization with predictive analytics to identify sites approaching capacity constraints before service quality degrades

Security Architecture for Distributed Edge Infrastructure

Edge data centers introduce new security challenges compared to centralized facilities. Physical security may be limited at unstaffed sites, network connectivity may traverse less secure pathways, and edge locations may be targeted by attackers seeking to compromise distributed infrastructure. We implement defense-in-depth security architectures tailored to edge environments:

Physical security includes biometric access control, video surveillance with cloud recording, and environmental sensors detecting unauthorized entry, temperature excursions, and water leaks. Edge servers use hardware root of trust (TPM, secure boot) to ensure only authenticated firmware and operating systems execute, preventing persistent malware infections.

Network segmentation isolates management traffic, customer workloads, and telco control plane traffic using VLANs, VRFs, and micro-segmentation. Zero-trust network access (ZTNA) replaces traditional VPNs for remote administration, requiring continuous authentication and authorization for every access session. All data in transit between edge sites and centralized management uses IPsec or TLS encryption to prevent eavesdropping and tampering.
